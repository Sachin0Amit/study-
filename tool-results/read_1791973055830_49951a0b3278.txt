     8→     8→# PART 1 — DIGITAL SAT (College Board, current adaptive format)
     9→     9→
    10→    10→The digital SAT measures two sections — **Reading & Writing** and **Math** — delivered in the Bluebook app via **multistage adaptive testing (MST)**. It replaced the paper SAT internationally (Mar 2023) and in the U.S. (Mar 2024). Every question is discrete (one question per passage / standalone math item).
    11→    11→
    12→    12→## 1. Math Section — 4 Content Domains (44 questions total, 70 minutes)
    13→    13→
    14→    14→Math domains, skills and testing points below are from the official College Board *Specifications Overview* (Table 3) and the official Student Question Bank skill descriptions.
    15→    15→
    16→    16→### Domain 1: Algebra — ≈35% of section, 13–15 questions
    17→    17→*Measures the ability to analyze, fluently solve, and create linear equations and inequalities, and to analyze and fluently solve equations and systems of equations using multiple techniques.*
    18→    18→
    19→    19→- **Skill 1.1 — Linear equations in one variable**
    20→    20→  - Solve single- and multi-step linear equations fluently
    21→    21→  - Create linear equations in one variable to solve problems in context
    22→    22→  - Interpret the structure of an equation: constants, variables, coefficients, terms, solutions
    23→    23→  - Identify equations with **no solution** or **infinitely many solutions**
    24→    24→  - Fluency with algebraic manipulation (combining like terms, distributing, moving terms)
    25→    25→- **Skill 1.2 — Linear equations in two variables**
    26→    26→  - Work with the form **Ax + By = C** (standard form)
    27→    27→  - Find the value of one variable when given the other
    28→    28→  - Make connections among algebraic, graphical, and tabular representations of a two-variable linear equation
    29→    29→  - Write the equation of a line; compute **slope**; interpret x- and y-**intercepts**
    30→    30→  - Identify **parallel** and **perpendicular** lines via slopes
    31→    31→  - Relate two variables to model a real-world relationship
    32→    32→- **Skill 1.3 — Linear functions**
    33→    33→  - Model relationships with linear functions; use function notation f(x)
    34→    34→  - Interpret **input/output pairs** and **rate of change** (slope as a rate in context)
    35→    35→  - Connect a function to its matching graph, table, or algebraic representation
    36→    36→  - Interpret constants, variables, factors, and terms of a linear function in context
    37→    37→- **Skill 1.4 — Systems of two linear equations in two variables**
    38→    38→  - Solve systems graphically and algebraically (**substitution, elimination**)
    39→    39→  - Determine when a system has **no solution, a unique solution, or infinitely many solutions**
    40→    40→  - Connect a system of equations to a matching graph (intersection of lines)
    41→    41→  - Create systems of two linear equations in two variables from context
    42→    42→- **Skill 1.5 — Linear inequalities in one or two variables**
    43→    43→  - Solve and interpret linear inequalities in one variable
    44→    44→  - Work with two-variable inequalities in the xy-plane; graph solution regions
    45→    45→  - Interpret a point in the xy-plane as a **possible solution** of an inequality (or system)
    46→    46→  - Make connections among tabular, algebraic, and graphical representations of inequalities
    47→    47→
    48→    48→### Domain 2: Advanced Math — ≈35% of section, 13–15 questions
    49→    49→*Measures skills and knowledge central for progression to more advanced math courses, including understanding of absolute value, quadratic, exponential, polynomial, rational, radical, and other nonlinear equations.*
    50→    50→
    51→    51→- **Skill 2.1 — Equivalent expressions**
    52→    52→  - Use algebraic structure and **properties of operations** to **factor, rewrite, add, subtract, and multiply polynomials**
    53→    53→  - Rewrite expressions with **rational exponents in radical form** (and vice versa)
    54→    54→  - Simplify/expand/rearrange expressions into required equivalent forms
    55→    55→  - Manipulate rational expressions and radicals to produce equivalent forms
    56→    56→- **Skill 2.2 — Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems of equations in two variables**
    57→    57→  - Solve **quadratic equations** (factoring, quadratic formula, completing the square)
    58→    58→  - Determine the **number of real solutions** of a quadratic (discriminant)
    59→    59→  - Solve **absolute value equations**
    60→    60→  - Solve **rational and radical equations** (including recognizing **extraneous solutions**)
    61→    61→  - Solve **polynomial equations** (factoring, roots)
    62→    62→  - Solve **systems of equations in two variables** where one or both equations are nonlinear (e.g., linear–quadratic, quadratic–quadratic)
    63→    63→  - Match the solutions of a system to a graph (points of intersection)
    64→    64→- **Skill 2.3 — Nonlinear functions**
    65→    65→  - **Quadratic functions**: interpret constants (vertex form, standard form); maximum/minimum values; zeros; transform/interpret graphs
    66→    66→  - **Exponential functions**: model growth and decay; interpret constant ratio/growth factor and initial value; interpret **asymptotes**; transform/interpret graphs
    67→    67→  - **Polynomial functions**: interpret input/output pairs, factors, terms; end behavior; zeros and their multiplicities
    68→    68→  - **Rational, radical, absolute value, and piecewise-defined functions**: interpret and transform graphs
    69→    69→  - Interpret the meaning of input/output pairs, constants, variables, factors, terms in a nonlinear model
    70→    70→  - Function concepts: domain and range, function notation, relating graphs/tables/equations, composition of a function with a constant/linear expression, identifying a function from a relationship
    71→    71→
    72→    72→### Domain 3: Problem-Solving and Data Analysis — ≈15% of section, 5–7 questions
    73→    73→*Measures the ability to apply quantitative reasoning about ratios, rates, and proportional relationships; understand and apply unit rate; and analyze and interpret one- and two-variable data.*
    74→    74→
    75→    75→- **Skill 3.1 — Ratios, rates, proportional relationships, and units**
    76→    76→  - Solve problems using a **proportional relationship** between quantities
    77→    77→  - Calculate/use **ratios and rates**; identify and use **unit rates**
    78→    78→  - Work with **units, derived units, and unit conversion**
    79→    79→  - Apply to **scale drawings** and problems in natural and social sciences
    80→    80→- **Skill 3.2 — Percentages**
    81→    81→  - Calculate percents; percent **increase/decrease**; discounts, interest, taxes, tips
    82→    82→  - Recognize the relationship between a percent increase/decrease and a **growth factor** (e.g., 1.05 = +5%)
    83→    83→- **Skill 3.3 — One-variable data: distributions and measures of center and spread**
    84→    84→  - Analyze distributions in **frequency tables, histograms, dot plots, and box plots**
    85→    85→  - Calculate/interpret **mean, median, mode** (measures of center)
    86→    86→  - **Range, interquartile range, standard deviation** (measures of spread)
    87→    87→  - Effect of **outliers**; compare multiple distributions
    88→    88→- **Skill 3.4 — Two-variable data: models and scatterplots**
    89→    89→  - Interpret **scatterplots**; describe associations (form, direction, strength, outliers)
    90→    90→  - Use/finterpret the **line of best fit**; interpolate and extrapolate
    91→    91→  - Fit and distinguish **linear, quadratic, and exponential models** to data in a scatterplot
    92→    92→  - Interpret a graph modeling a relationship between two quantities
    93→    93→- **Skill 3.5 — Probability and conditional probability**
    94→    94→  - Calculate probability of an event from **tables, area models, or descriptions** of situations
    95→    95→  - **Independent events**; **conditional probability** (including via two-way tables)
    96→    96→- **Skill 3.6 — Inference from sample statistics and margin of error**
    97→    97→  - Use **sample mean and sample proportion** to estimate **population mean and population proportion**
    98→    98→  - Interpret **margin of error**; understand that a **larger sample size generally leads to a smaller margin of error**
    99→    99→- **Skill 3.7 — Evaluating statistical claims: observational studies and experiments**
   100→   100→  - Determine whether there is evidence for a **causal relationship** from a study description
   101→   101→  - Understand why **random assignment** provides evidence for causation
   102→   102→  - Identify which **population** results from random samples can be generalized to
   103→   103→  - Distinguish **observational studies** from **controlled experiments**; confounding variables
   104→   104→
   105→   105→### Domain 4: Geometry and Trigonometry — ≈15% of section, 5–7 questions
   106→   106→*Measures the ability to solve problems focusing on area and volume; lines, angles, and triangles; right triangles and trigonometry; and circles. (PSAT 8/9 omits trigonometry.)*
   107→   107→
   108→   108→- **Skill 4.1 — Area and volume**
   109→   109→  - Solve area/perimeter problems: triangles, rectangles, squares, parallelograms, trapezoids, circles, composite figures
   110→   110→  - Surface area and volume: prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones, spheres (formulas provided on the reference sheet)
   111→   111→  - Use **scale factors** to compute changes to length, area, and volume
   112→   112→- **Skill 4.2 — Lines, angles, and triangles**
   113→   113→  - **Triangle sum theorem**; exterior angle theorem
   114→   114→  - **Vertical angle theorem**; angles formed by a **transversal cutting parallel lines**
   115→   115→  - **Congruence and similarity** of triangles (congruence criteria: SSS, SAS, ASA; AA similarity)
   116→   116→  - Understand how scale factor changes lengths but not angles
   117→   117→  - Solve problems using similarity ratios/side proportions
   118→   118→- **Skill 4.3 — Right triangles and trigonometry**
   119→   119→  - **Pythagorean theorem** (and common Pythagorean triples)
   120→   120→  - **Special right triangles**: 45°–45°–90° and 30°–60°–90° (given on reference sheet)
   121→   121→  - Right-triangle **trigonometric ratios** (sine, cosine, tangent) — solve for sides/angles in similar triangles
   122→   122→  - Relationship between the **sine and cosine of complementary angles** (sin x = cos(90° − x))
   123→   123→  - Convert angle measures between **degrees and radians** (π radians = 180°)
   124→   124→  - **Unit circle** basics (coordinates of points; quadrant signs)
   125→   125→- **Skill 4.4 — Circles**
   126→   126→  - Definitions/properties: **radius, diameter, chord, tangent, secant**; tangent ⊥ radius
   127→   127→  - **Central and inscribed angles**; arc measure; **arc length**; **sector area**
   128→   128→  - Convert between degrees and radians; use the unit circle
   129→   129→  - **Circle equations**: standard form **(x − h)² + (y − k)² = r²**; find center/radius; **complete the square** to convert to standard form; create and interpret graphs of circles
   130→   130→  - **Distance formula** in the coordinate plane (and connections to the Pythagorean theorem)
   131→   131→  - Work with ordered pairs on a circle
   132→   132→
   133→   133→**Math item format notes (official):** ≈75% four-option multiple choice and ≈25% **student-produced response (SPR / grid-in)**; calculator allowed on the entire section (built-in **Desmos graphing calculator** in Bluebook, or an approved personal calculator); a **formula reference sheet** is provided; ≈30% of questions are in-context ("word") problems set in science, social science, or real-world scenarios; questions in each module are ordered easiest → hardest; all four domains appear in each module.
   134→   134→
   135→   135→## 2. Reading & Writing Section — 4 Content Domains (54 questions total, 64 minutes)
   136→   136→
   137→   137→Each question is a discrete, four-option multiple-choice item attached to one short passage (25–150 words) or a pair of passages. Passages represent **literature, history/social studies, the humanities, and science**. Informational graphics (tables, bar graphs, line graphs) appear in some questions. Domain/skill breakdown below is from the official *Specifications Overview* (Table 2) and Student Question Bank.
   138→   138→
   139→   139→### Domain 1: Craft and Structure — ≈28% of section, 13–15 questions
   140→   140→*Measures comprehension, vocabulary, analysis, synthesis, and reasoning skills and knowledge needed to understand and use high-utility words and phrases in context, evaluate texts rhetorically, and make connections between topically related texts.*
   141→   141→
   142→   142→- **Skill 1.1 — Words in Context**
   143→   143→  - Determine the most logical and **precise word/phrase** to complete a text (vocabulary in context)
   144→   144→  - "As used in the text, what does ___ most nearly mean?" (high-utility academic vocabulary)
   145→   145→  - Textually consistent word choice across contexts (multiple meanings/nuance)
   146→   146→- **Skill 1.2 — Text Structure and Purpose**
   147→   147→  - Identify the **main purpose** of the overall text
   148→   148→  - Describe the **function of an underlined sentence/claim** within the text as a whole
   149→   149→  - Analyze the **overall structure/organization** of a text and rhetorical strategies used
   150→   150→- **Skill 1.3 — Cross-Text Connections** (paired passages)
   151→   151→  - Identify points of **agreement** between two authors/passages
   152→   152→  - Determine how the author of one passage would most likely **respond to a claim** in the other
   153→   153→  - Compare similarities/differences in perspective, emphasis, or interpretation between texts
   154→   154→
   155→   155→### Domain 2: Information and Ideas — ≈26% of section, 12–14 questions
   156→   156→*Measures comprehension, analysis, and reasoning skills and knowledge, and the ability to locate, interpret, evaluate, and integrate information and ideas from texts and informational graphics (tables, bar graphs, and line graphs).*
   157→   157→
   158→   158→- **Skill 2.1 — Central Ideas and Details**
   159→   159→  - Identify the **main idea/theme** of a text
   160→   160→  - Locate and interpret **supporting details**; answer questions about specific details in the passage
   161→   161→  - Select an accurate **summary** of the text
   162→   162→- **Skill 2.2 — Command of Evidence**
   163→   163→  - **Textual evidence**: identify which finding/claim/quotation would best **support a conclusion** about the text
   164→   164→  - **Quantitative evidence**: interpret **tables, bar graphs, line graphs**; determine which data support/illustrate a claim; complete a table to be consistent with findings
   165→   165→- **Skill 2.3 — Inferences**
   166→   166→  - "Which choice **most logically completes** the text?" — draw valid inferences beyond literal comprehension
   167→   167→  - Complete incomplete ideas consistent with textual evidence
   168→   168→
   169→   169→### Domain 3: Standard English Conventions — ≈26% of section, 11–15 questions
   170→   170→*Measures the ability to edit text to conform to core conventions of Standard English sentence structure, usage, and punctuation. All "Which choice completes the text so that it conforms to the conventions of Standard English?" items; arranged easiest → hardest regardless of convention tested.*
   171→   171→
   172→   172→- **Skill 3.1 — Boundaries** (options differ in punctuation)
   173→   173→  - **Sentence boundaries**: recognizing complete sentences vs. fragments and run-ons
   174→   174→  - **Linking clauses**: coordination (FANBOYS + comma), subordination (dependent markers), semicolon joining independent clauses
   175→   175→  - **Punctuation within/among sentences**: commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, apostrophes
   176→   176→  - **Supplements**: correct punctuation of nonessential/parenthetical material (names, appositives, interrupters)
   177→   177→  - Unwanted punctuation at clause boundaries
   178→   178→- **Skill 3.2 — Form, Structure, and Sense** (options differ in words or sentence structures)
   179→   179→  - **Subject–verb agreement** (including with intervening phrases; indefinite pronouns)
   180→   180→  - **Verb tense, mood, and voice** (consistency; conditional)
   181→   181→  - **Pronoun–antecedent agreement and clarity**; pronoun case
   182→   182→  - **Possessives** (singular/plural) vs. plurals vs. contractions
   183→   183→  - **Noun forms** (count vs. non-count), adjective vs. adverb forms, comparative/superlative forms
   184→   184→  - **Modifier placement** (dangling/misplaced modifiers)
   185→   185→  - Frequently confused word pairs (e.g., its/it's, their/there); logical sense and idiomatic usage
   186→   186→  - Logical conjunctions/transitions linking structure within sentences
   187→   187→
   188→   188→### Domain 4: Expression of Ideas — ≈20% of section, 8–12 questions
   189→   189→*Measures the ability to revise texts to improve the effectiveness of written expression and to meet specific rhetorical goals.*
   190→   190→
   191→   191→- **Skill 4.1 — Transitions**
   192→   192→  - Select the most logical **transition word/phrase** to complete a text (addition, cause-effect, contrast, exemplification, emphasis, sequence/chronology, summary)
   193→   193→- **Skill 4.2 — Rhetorical Synthesis**
   194→   194→  - Effectively use information from a **student's notes** (bullet-point information) to accomplish a stated **rhetorical goal**
   195→   195→  - Sentence combining/revision for **concision, precision, and rhetorical effect**; eliminate redundancy
   196→   196→
   197→   197→**Domain order within each R&W module (official):** Craft and Structure questions come first, followed by Information and Ideas, then Standard English Conventions, then Expression of Ideas; within C&S, I&I, and EoI, similar skills are grouped and ordered easiest → hardest.

   199→   199→## 3. Digital SAT Exam Pattern (official structure)
   200→   200→
   201→   201→| Component | Reading & Writing | Math |
   202→   202→|---|---|---|
   203→   203→| Modules | 2 (32 minutes each) | 2 (35 minutes each) |
   204→   204→| Questions | 54 total (27/module; 25 operational + 2 pretest per module) | 44 total (22/module; 20 operational + 2 pretest per module) |
   205→   205→| Time | 64 minutes | 70 minutes |
   206→   206→| Question types | Discrete four-option MCQ (1 per passage) | ≈75% four-option MCQ + ≈25% student-produced response |
   207→   207→| Graphics | Tables, bar graphs, line graphs | Yes (science/social science/real-world contexts ≈30%) |
   208→   208→| Tools | — | Built-in graphing calculator (all of Math), formula reference sheet |
   209→   209→| Passages | 25–150 words; literature, history/social studies, humanities, science; sometimes passage pairs | — |
   210→   210→
   211→   211→- **Total:** 98 questions, **2 hours 14 minutes** (2h 4m of section time + 10-minute break between R&W and Math)
   212→   212→- **Adaptive format:** Section-level **multistage adaptive testing (MST)** — Module 1 of each section has a broad mix of easy, medium, hard questions; based on Module 1 performance, Module 2 is *more difficult* or *less difficult*. Students can move freely among questions within a module (flag/mark for review, navigate back/forth), but cannot return to a previous module.
   213→   213→- **Scoring:** Each section scored **200–800** in 10-point intervals; **total score 400–1600** (sum of two section scores). No subscores or cross-test scores on the digital SAT. Vertical scale links SAT/PSAT suite. College & Career Readiness Benchmarks: **R&W 480, Math 530**.
   214→   214→- **Delivery:** Bluebook digital testing app (laptop/iPad/managed device; device-lending available); scores in days; the app provides Mark for Review, testing timer, calculator, reference sheet, annotation tools; small number of indistinguishable unscored (pretest) questions in each module.
   215→   215→- **Question order:** All four domains appear in each module; questions arranged easiest → hardest (per domain groups in R&W; across the whole module in Math).

   246→   246→- **Section B — Reasoning & Problem Solving (≈20 Q)**
   247→   247→  - Analogies (word/number/letter); classification (odd one out)
   248→   248→  - Series (number, letter, figure); coding–decoding
   249→   249→  - Blood relations; direction & distance sense
   250→   250→  - Syllogisms; statement–assumption/conclusion; logical deduction
   251→   251→  - Seating arrangements & puzzles; Venn diagrams
   252→   252→  - Order & ranking; calendar & clock problems; non-verbal/figure reasoning
   253→   253→- **Section C — General Awareness (≈20 Q)**
   254→   254→  - Current affairs (national & international)
   255→   255→  - Indian history, polity & constitution, geography, economy
   256→   256→  - Science & technology in news; defence & security developments, DRDO missions/products
   257→   257→  - Awards, honours, sports; books & authors; important days; organizations (UN, ISRO, DRDO, HAL, BARC…)
   258→   258→- **Section D — English Language (≈20 Q)**
   259→   259→  - Grammar: articles, prepositions, tenses, subject–verb agreement, error spotting
   260→   260→  - Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitution, idioms & phrases
   261→   261→  - Sentence completion/jumbled sentences; active–passive & direct–indirect (basic)
   262→   262→  - Reading comprehension (short passages)
   263→   263→- **Section E — General Science (≈20 Q)**
   264→   264→  - Physics (10+2 level: mechanics, heat, optics, electricity, modern physics basics)
   265→   265→  - Chemistry (10+2 level: atomic structure, bonding, acids/bases, metals/non-metals, carbon compounds basics)
   266→   266→  - Biology (cells, genetics basics, human physiology, plant biology, ecology)
   267→   267→  - Everyday science; recent S&T developments; space & defence science

   271→   271→## 2. Tier II — Discipline Syllabi (descriptive papers; per official RAC descriptive syllabus ≈ UPSC ESE-Mains style, as documented for Advt 140 written exam)
   272→   272→
   273→   273→> The 2022 RAC written exam consisted of **Paper 1 (300 marks, 3 h)** and **Paper 2 (300 marks, 3 h)**, descriptive. The topic lists below are the official discipline syllabi (as published by RAC and reproduced by Testbook/Adda247/Embibe).
   274→   274→
   275→   275→### 2.1 Computer Science & Engineering (CSE)
   276→   276→
   277→   277→**Paper 1 (CSE):**
   278→   278→- **Engineering Mathematics**
   279→   279→  - *Linear Algebra:* Matrices, determinants, system of linear equations, eigenvalues and eigenvectors, LU decomposition, orthonormal bases; Transforms — Fourier, DCT, wavelets
   280→   280→  - *Calculus:* Limits, continuity, differentiability, maxima and minima, mean value theorem, integration; Vector calculus — gradient, divergence, curl, integral theorems

   387→   387→## 3. DRDO Scientist B — Exam Pattern Summary (all routes)
   388→   388→
   389→   389→**Legacy Tier-I + Tier-II route (the structure requested for the platform):**
   390→   390→- **Tier-I (Screening):** objective test, ~100 marks, ~2 hours; 5 sections (~20 questions each): Quantitative Aptitude, Reasoning & Problem Solving, General Awareness, English Language, General Science; **negative marking 0.25 per wrong answer**; screening/qualifying in nature — used to shortlist for Tier-II
   391→   391→- **Tier-II (Descriptive):** ~3 hours, **75 marks** = **60 marks engineering-discipline** descriptive questions + **15 marks** research aptitude (science & technology priorities, research aptitude, quantitative aptitude & reasoning)
   392→   392→- **Tier-III — Personal Interview:** final stage; in later RAC ads the final merit used **80% weightage of written (Tier-II/descriptive) + 20% weightage of interview**; interviews require minimum qualifying marks — **70% (UR) / 60% (EWS/OBC/SC/ST/PwD)** (per official Advt 140/156 criteria)
   393→   393→- Eligibility essentials: first-class B.E./B.Tech (or M.Sc. for science posts) in the advertised discipline + **valid GATE score** (or top IIT/NIT marks ≥80% in some ads); age limit ≤28 years (UR, DRDO) with category relaxations (OBC 31, SC/ST 33 etc.)
   394→   394→
   395→   395→**Advt 140 (2022) written-exam route (ECE/ME/CSE):** GATE-based shortlisting (1:25) → offline **descriptive written exam: 2 papers × 300 marks, 3 hours each (600 total)**, syllabus per UPSC ESE (Mains) in the discipline → interview (shortlist 1:5 from written merit) → final merit = 80% written + 20% interview; interview qualifying 70%/60%.
   396→   396→
   397→   397→**Current route (Advt 156, 2025):** shortlisting purely on **valid GATE score (2023/2024/2025)** → **Personal Interview** → final merit = **80% GATE + 20% interview**; interview qualifying 70% UR / 60% reserved. No negative marking concept (no CBT); medical examination & character-antecedent verification precede appointment (Level-10, 7th CPC pay matrix, basic pay ₹56,100).
   398→   398→
   399→   399→**CEPTAM STA-'B' (comparison):** Tier-I CBT 150 Q/150 marks/120 min (negative 0.25; qualifying 40%/35%) + Tier-II subject CBT 120 Q/120 marks/120 min (negative 0.25) + 20-minute personality test (qualifying).
   400→   400→
   401→   401→**Other DRDO scientist recruitment channels (context):** ROSSA (Registration of Students with Scholarships and Aptitude — for high-GATE/merit fresh candidates), campus/Institute-level selection for young scientists, lateral entry for experienced professionals, and DRDO Research Fellowships (JRF/SRF/RA) at laboratories.
